# Marrowcraft

**Marrowcraft**, or, more precisely and more rarely used outside the trade itself, the distinction between **marrow-work** and **vein-work**. The general term for the craft, part mining, part alchemy, part quietly-managed theology, of drawing usable substance from Vaelkyr's remains and turning it into fuel, medicine, and, in its rarest and most dangerous form, something that behaves like a miracle. Not practiced anywhere else in the world, for the simple reason that nowhere else has a body this size, this old, or this reluctant to finish dying.

---

### What Outsiders Assume

A foreign buyer hearing "ichor" for the first time tends to imagine something closer to a potion: bottled, uniform, reliably the same working every time a stopper comes out. Vaelmourn's own physicians find this assumption almost sweet in its wrongness. Marrow is closer to a resource than a reagent, harvested in bulk and refined the way tallow or ore is refined, its quality varying cavern to cavern the way a vintner's grapes vary field to field. Ichor is not a potion at all. It is not made, in any sense a foreign alchemist would recognize; it is found, already finished, by something that stopped being alive a very long time ago, and every drop a Marrowcrafter draws off is a fixed, non-renewing quantity of a thing nobody on the continent knows how to replace. The two substances come from the same body and are handled, on the ground, by many of the same guilds. They are not, in any other sense, the same craft.

---

## 1. Two Substances, One Body

| | Marrow | Ichor |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Preserved bone-marrow, dense and slow-burning, filling cavities throughout the larger bones | Vaelkyr's own preserved blood, sealed in isolated veins, concentrated near the Skull |
| **Abundance** | Common; entire caverns of it, continuously worked | Vanishingly rare; a vein, once tapped dry, does not refill |
| **Magic content** | Low, ambient, residual; enough to make refined marrow medicinally potent but not dangerous to handle | Extremely high; effectively raw divine potential, still active |
| **Primary handlers** | Marrow-miners, renderers, apothecaries | Vein-tenders, Choir-licensed physicians |
| **Governing body** | Local hold custom, informally overseen by the Choir | The Choir directly, with binding authority over access |
| **Characteristic risk** | Ordinary mining hazards: cave-in, bad air, fatigue | **The Hollow** |

The distinction locals draw is not chemical so much as devotional. Marrow is understood as what a body leaves behind after using it; ichor is understood as what a body was still, in some sense, actively doing when it stopped.

## 2. Harvesting

### Marrow-Mining

Marrow-caverns are worked exactly like any other mine: access shafts sunk along a bone's length, timber or ribwood shoring where the cavity is wide enough to need it, and haul-lines running crude marrow up to the surface in sealed casks before it has a chance to cool and set. The work is steady. A productive cavern, once found, can supply a hold for a generation, and marrow-mining guilds pass caverns down through apprenticeship. The one universal rule, observed without exception across every hold, is that no marrow-cavern is worked without first being formally logged with the nearest Choir chapter-house, not because the Choir owns the marrow, but because an unlogged cavern is an unlogged void in a bone that entire towns are, quite literally, standing on.

### Vein-Tapping

Ichor-veins are found far more rarely than marrow-caverns, most often accidentally, by marrow-miners cutting into a cavity that turns out to run wet rather than dense, and every confirmed find is reported to the Choir within days, since working an unblessed vein is one of the few acts on Vaelmourn that draws continent-wide disapproval regardless of which hold you're standing in. A **vein-tender**, a role that takes the better part of a decade to be trusted with, does the actual tapping: a narrow bore drilled at the vein's lowest accessible point, fitted with a bronze or bone-set tap that can be closed the instant flow becomes visible, and never left open longer than a single supervised draw requires. Ichor does not flow freely the way blood would from a living wound; it seeps, slowly, and a vein-tender's entire training is built around the discipline of taking only what a session calls for and stopping, reliably, well before the vein's own slow pressure would offer more.

## 3. The Distillation Process

### Rendering Marrow

Crude marrow, as hauled up from the cavern, is not usable directly; it is rendered, a process closer to candlemaking than alchemy, in long, low-fired vats kept at a controlled simmer for a full day and night. The process yields three products, skimmed off in sequence as the vat settles:

- **Marrow-tallow**, the bulk of the yield, a dense, slow-burning fuel used continent-wide for heating and lighting.
- **Bone-salts**, a fine mineral residue settling at the vat's base, ground and used as a base binder in nearly every marrow-derived medicine that follows.
- **Marrow-spirit**, a thin, faintly warm oil skimmed from the surface, the only one of the three products with any real magical potency, and the ingredient apothecaries build remedies from.

A rendering-house's reputation rests almost entirely on the purity of its marrow-spirit skim, and a good renderer, one who knows exactly when a vat has simmered long enough without simmering past the point where the spirit's potency starts to burn off, commands wages closer to a bonewright's than a laborer's.

### Cutting Ichor

Ichor is never rendered; it is far too potent, and far too scarce, to risk any process that assumes a margin for error. Instead it is **cut**: mixed, in exactingly small measured ratios, with a stabilizing base, most often refined marrow-spirit itself, so that a working physician is handling a dilute, manageable solution rather than raw ichor at any point after the vein-tender's own careful draw. The ratio is not fixed; it is judged session by session by a Choir-trained apothecary reading the ichor's own color and weight, since a vein's yield varies subtly by depth and proximity to the Skull. Undercutting produces a working too weak to reliably help; overcutting, using ichor closer to its raw state than a given working calls for, is the single most common cause of a bad outcome, for the physician handling it as much as the patient receiving it.

## 4. Medical Uses

### Marrow-Derived Remedies

The bulk of Vaelmourn's medicine, and a fair share of its export trade, runs on marrow-spirit and bone-salt preparations: analgesic salves for joint and bone pain, restorative tonics prescribed for wasting illness and slow recovery from serious injury, and a well-regarded bone-knitting paste that Vaelmourn apothecaries claim, with real justification, sets a clean fracture faster than anything a foreign physician can offer. None of this is miraculous. It is simply good, reliable, well-understood medicine, made from a base ingredient that happens to be mildly magical, and most Vaelmourn households keep a jar of basic marrow-salve the unremarkable way another people might keep a bottle of willow-tincture.

### Ichor-Derived Remedies

Cut ichor is where Vaelmourn medicine stops being merely good and starts being inexplicable. A properly cut and administered dose has been documented closing wounds that should have been fatal within the hour, arresting the progress of illnesses no other treatment on the continent touches, and, in the oldest and best-attested cases, restoring function to limbs and organs long since given up as lost. The continent's most famous healing miracles trace back, almost without exception, to a named physician with confirmed access to a specific vein, and Vaelmourn medical history is told, in large part, as a history of which vein was open, and to whom, during which crisis.

It is not, however, reliable in the way ordinary medicine is reliable. Two doses cut from the same vein on different days can behave differently, response varies patient to patient for reasons no apothecary has ever fully mapped, and a treatment that saved one patient's life has done nothing at all for the next. Vaelmourn physicians treat this unpredictability the way the Choir treats the larger question of Vaelkyr's godhood: as a fact to be worked around rather than solved, and a licensed ichor-physician's actual skill lies less in any fixed dosing formula than in reading, session by session, whether a given cut and a given patient are likely to answer each other at all.

## 5. The Cost: The Hollow

Exposure to raw or lightly-cut ichor is never free, for the physician handling it as much as the patient receiving a heavy dose, and the cost is a condition universally known as **the Hollow**: a creeping numbness, first to minor bodily sensation, then to appetite, then, in advanced and thankfully rare cases, to emotion itself, that mirrors, closely enough to unsettle most theologians, what long-dead flesh presumably feels like from the inside.

| Stage | Presentation | Typical cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Chill** | Cold fingers and lips that don't warm properly for a day or two after handling; universal, minor, expected | A single ordinary session, cutting or administering a standard dose |
| **Numbing** | Reduced sensation to touch and temperature lasting a week or more; loss of taste noted in some cases | Repeated sessions too close together, or one significantly overcut dose |
| **The Deep Hollow** | Sustained emotional flattening, difficulty forming ordinary attachment, a reported sense of watching one's own life from slightly outside it | Sustained overexposure, most often chronic professional overwork rather than any single incident |

There is no known cure for the Deep Hollow beyond time, distance from further exposure and simply living long enough for it to fade the way any old wound eventually fades. Most holds that work an ichor-vein rotate access to it deliberately among their vein-tenders and physicians, treating overuse less as an occupational hazard to be managed than as a mild, cumulative trespass against whatever is left of Vaelkyr to be trespassed against, and a physician who insists on taking more sessions than their rotation allows is regarded less as ambitious than as someone who has stopped being able to tell they're already partway Hollow.

## 6. Grading, Rationing, and the Choir

The Choir's authority over Marrowcraft is informal in the way most Vaelmourn authority is informal, no army, no law past custom, and near-total in practice regardless. Every confirmed ichor-vein is catalogued, blessed or withheld from blessing, and assigned a rotation the moment it's reported, and a vein worked outside that rotation produces ichor no reputable buyer, local or foreign, will touch, since goods drawn from an unblessed site carry a stigma that follows them straight onto a foreign ship's manifest. This gives the Choir, in effect, total control over the entire ichor supply without the Choir ever having to claim ownership of a single vein outright, and most Vaelmourn holds prefer it that way: nobody wants the argument over who actually owns a piece of a dead god, and letting the Choir hold the ledger instead of any one city has kept that argument from ever needing to happen.

Marrow is graded far more casually, by rendering-house reputation and simple buyer experience, but ichor is graded formally, on a scale the Choir itself maintains, running from **thin** (barely distinguishable from well-cut marrow-spirit in effect) through **true** (the working grade, used in most licensed medicine) to **deep** (drawn near the Skull itself, reserved for the gravest cases, and released only with a Choir physician present). A buyer, local or foreign, who claims to be holding deep-grade ichor without a Choir attestation to show for it is either lying or holding something considerably more dangerous than they realize.

---

## 7. Ichor Abroad

Ichor sold abroad is, without exception, thin or true grade, pre-cut, and accompanied by a Choir attestation of its source vein and draw-date, since an uncertified vial reaching a foreign port is worth a fraction of a certified one and considerably harder to sell at all. Foreign physicians who acquire it report a working potency that fades noticeably faster once it's left the continent, an effect Choir scholars attribute to distance from whatever residual presence still keeps the rest of Vaelkyr's remains from behaving like ordinary bone, and most reputable foreign buyers know to use a shipment quickly rather than let it sit. A handful of Kyrai and old Vhonoris alchemists have attempted, over the centuries, to synthesize something ichor-like from other reagents; none has ever produced more than a very expensive placebo, and Vaelmourn physicians, asked about these efforts, tend to respond with something close to pity rather than professional alarm.
