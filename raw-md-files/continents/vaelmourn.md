# Vaelmourn, the Bonelands

**Vaelmourn**, or simply **the Bonelands**, the name used almost universally, including by the people who actually live there, the formal name being reserved mostly for treaties, hymnals, and the occasional pompous merchant's letterhead. Named for **Vaelkyr**, the dragon believed by nearly every faith that acknowledges the continent's existence to have been, in truth, a god, or near enough to one that the distinction stopped mattering the day it died. A landmass raised not on rock, but on the fossilized, still-standing remains of the single largest creature ever to have existed, in the world's northeast, where its corpse has lain, slowly becoming a continent, for longer than any calendar in current use can account for.

---

### Geological Background

No one alive witnessed Vaelkyr's death, and no myth agrees on how it happened, only that it did, and that the body did not simply rot away the way a smaller creature's would. Instead, over some incalculable span of time, soil gathered in the hollows of its frame, rain filled the basin of its ribcage, and life, ordinary and otherwise, took root on a scale that eventually made the difference between "corpse" and "continent" a matter of perspective rather than fact. The bones did not decay so much as **weather**, the way a mountain range weathers, and what's left today functions, geologically, exactly like the terrain it resembles: the ribs are mountain ranges, the spine is a continental ridge, and people build towns on them the same unthinking way they'd build on any other high ground, difference being that the ground, here, used to be alive.

## 1. Anatomy as Geography

Vaelmourn's terrain maps, with uncanny and occasionally inconvenient literalness, onto the animal it once was.

| Region | Anatomical Origin | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **The Spine** | The vertebral column | A single unbroken mountain ridge running the continent's full length, its individual peaks understood locally as vertebrae; the seat of Vaelmourn's oldest and highest settlements. |
| **The Ribcage** | The ribs and the space they enclose | A vast, sheltered interior basin, walled on all sides by rib-ranges some so tall their upper reaches vanish into permanent cloud cover; the continent's richest farmland and densest population. |
| **The Skull** | The head | A single enormous headland jutting into the northern sea, its eye sockets now two deep, sheltered bays and its jaw a natural harbor famous, and mildly infamous, for teeth still large enough to serve as freestanding towers. |
| **The Wingfields** | The folded wing-bones | A high, wind-scoured plateau of long, fanning ridgelines, sparsely settled, prized for grazing and for a very particular kind of view. |
| **The Tailreach** | The tail | A long, narrowing peninsula breaking apart, at its far end, into a scatter of small vertebral islands, the continent's frontier and its main gateway to sea trade. |

### The Ribs Themselves

Individual ribs are wide enough, in their thickest stretches, to hold entire towns along their length, and rib-top settlements are Vaelmourn's most iconic and most depicted (by visiting cartographers, at least) feature: narrow, elevated communities strung out along a single curving bone, with the sheer drop to the basin floor on one side and, on clear days, an uninterrupted view of a dozen sister-ribs marching off into the distance on the other. The tallest ribs' upper reaches are cold, thin-aired, and permanently mist-wrapped; no settlement has ever been built past a certain known height on any of them, not for lack of trying, but because no one has ever climbed high enough to find out where the rib actually ends.

## 2. Peoples and Settlement

Vaelmourn's current inhabitants are not descended from any people native to Vaelkyr's life; they are settlers, arrived over some six or seven centuries by the best local reckoning, drawn first by pilgrimage and later, more durably, by the continent's remarkable resources. What began as scattered shrine-camps at the base of the Spine has grown into a proper, if idiosyncratic, civilization: a few dozen sizable bone-built cities, hundreds of smaller rib-top and basin towns, and a shared culture built, unavoidably, around the fact that everyone's house, road, and well ultimately rests on the body of something that used to be alive, and that a great many locals still consider, in some sense, present.

### The Bonewrights

The continent's signature craft tradition is **bonewrighting**, the structural carving, joining, and hollowing of Vaelkyr's own remains into livable architecture. A skilled bonewright can read a section of bone the way a shipwright reads timber grain, and the trade carries real prestige: a bonewright's guild-mark is cut discreetly into every major structure they complete, and some of the oldest surviving guild-marks, on the Spine's original shrine-halls, are now considered sacred objects in their own right. Wood and quarried stone are both available on Vaelmourn and used constantly for ordinary construction; bone is reserved, by custom rather than law, for anything meant to last, or to matter.

## 3. Ichor and Marrow

### Marrow

The interior of Vaelmourn's larger bones, especially along the Spine and the deepest ribs, still holds enormous cavities of preserved marrow, dense, faintly warm, and slow-burning, which locals harvest as both fuel and, refined, a base ingredient in a wide range of medicines. Marrow-caverns are worked much like mines, tunneled into from access shafts sunk along a bone's length, and marrow-miners are a respected, well-compensated trade, in part because the work, while not especially dangerous, does require an unusual comfort with spending long shifts inside what is, undeniably, still a body.

### Ichor

Far rarer, and far more valuable, is **ichor**: pockets of Vaelkyr's own preserved blood, found sealed in isolated veins throughout the skeleton, especially concentrated near the Skull. Ichor is intensely magical, effectively raw divine potential still slowly leaking out of a source that, by any ordinary reckoning, should have run dry centuries ago, and it is prized abroad, when it can be gotten at all, for workings no ordinary reagent can match: the oldest and best-attested healing miracles on the continent trace back to a physician with access to an ichor-vein, and Vaelmourn's export economy runs disproportionately on the tiny, carefully rationed amount that ever reaches a ship.

Ichor is not without cost to draw on. Overexposure produces a condition locally called **the Hollow**, a creeping numbness to ordinary sensation and emotion that mirrors, a little too closely for most theologians' comfort, what long-dead flesh presumably feels like from the inside. Most Vaelmourn communities that work an ichor-vein rotate access to it deliberately, treating overuse less as an occupational hazard and more as a mild form of trespass against whatever is left of Vaelkyr to be trespassed against.

## 4. Faith and the Choir

### Is a Dead God Still a God

Theology on Vaelmourn is dominated by a single, genuinely unresolved question: whether Vaelkyr, having died, remains a god in any meaningful sense, or has become something else, a sacred site rather than a sacred being, holy the way a shrine is holy rather than the way a living deity is. Both positions have serious, long-established followings, and the argument between them is closer to friendly, ongoing civic debate than to anything like a holy war; most ordinary Vaelmourn residents hold a comfortably unexamined mix of both views without much difficulty.

### The Choir

The closest thing Vaelmourn has to a continent-spanning institution is **the Choir**, a loose, non-hierarchical association of scholar-clergy headquartered at the Spine's oldest shrine-city, who maintain the old pilgrim roads, catalog newly discovered marrow-caverns and ichor-veins, and formally mediate disputes over who has the right to work a given bone. The Choir has no army and very little direct political power, individual cities and rib-holds govern themselves, but its blessing (or withheld blessing) of a new mining claim carries enormous informal weight, since almost no local buyer wants goods drawn from a site the Choir has publicly declined to bless.

## 5. Flora and Fauna

Centuries of soil, rain, and residual divine magic pooling in Vaelkyr's frame have produced an ecosystem found nowhere else, distinct enough that Vaelmourn's naturalists (and a fair number of very determined foreign ones) treat the continent as a lifetime's worth of study on its own.

- **Grave-orchids**, luminous, faintly warm flowers that grow almost exclusively along old bone-seams, are the continent's signature export flora, prized abroad for perfumery and, among more superstitious buyers, as a ward against nightmares.
- **Ribwood**, a slow-growing, unusually pale timber, grows best rooted directly into bone rather than soil, and Vaelmourn carpenters consider ribwood grown on the Spine itself to be the finest, and most expensive, timber on the continent.
- **Bonewalkers**, large, docile scavenger beasts descended, most naturalists agree, from whatever originally fed on Vaelkyr's remains before the land stabilized, still roam the deeper Ribcage basin in small herds; long since harmless to people and now mostly kept, semi-domesticated, as pack animals suited to the continent's uneven terrain.
- **The deep marrow-caverns** host their own blind, pale ecosystem entirely independent of sunlight, fed on residual marrow-warmth: fungal groves the size of small forests, and a handful of cavern-dwelling creatures still only partially catalogued, given how few naturalists are willing to spend the necessary weeks that far inside a dead god to properly describe them.

## 6. Economy and Trade

Vaelmourn's export economy runs on three pillars: **marrow-fuel and its medicinal derivatives**, sold widely and in real bulk; **ichor**, sold in vanishingly small, extraordinarily expensive quantities to buyers who can afford it and are trusted not to misuse it; and **bonecraft goods**, furniture, tools, and decorative carving made from ribwood and worked bone, popular abroad as much for their genuine quality as for the simple fact of where they're from.

A fourth, informal pillar is **pilgrimage tourism**: a steady stream of religious travelers, scholars, and the merely curious arrive every season to walk the old shrine-roads up the Spine, and the towns along the main pilgrim route have built an entire secondary economy around hosting them, respectfully, and relieving them of a reasonable amount of coin along the way.

---

## 7. Governance

Vaelmourn has never had, and shows no particular interest in acquiring, a single continental government. Political authority sits with individual city- and rib-holds, each self-governing, loosely federated with their nearest neighbors along a shared bone or basin, and bound together more by the Choir's informal mediating presence and a shared, genuine reverence for the ground everyone's standing on than by any formal treaty. Disputes between holds, over a newly opened marrow-cavern or a contested pilgrim-road toll, are common, usually minor, and resolved, more often than not, by Choir arbitration, precedent, or the simple, practical fact that nobody wants to be remembered as the hold that started a war on top of a dead god.
