# Kyre

**Kyre**, the native name for the continent, its peoples, and its language alike, used the same way by the **Kyrai** who have always lived there. Called, by the various foreign powers that now hold its coasts, **the Continent of Beasts**, deliberately never anything more formal than that; a name is, in the reasoning of most of the occupying powers, a courtesy owed to a nation, and by their own official position, Kyre has never had one. The continent's other defining feature, a magic-dense crystalline mineral the Kyrai call **kyrite**, is known abroad almost exclusively by its trade name, **manastone**, a term chosen, deliberately, to describe the substance without describing the place or people it comes from.

---

### Geological Background

Kyre is a temperate, densely forested continent in the world's east, its interior dominated by old-growth woodland, broken uplands, and a long central mountain spine, the **Kyresh Range**, riddled with the mineral deposits that give the continent its second, more famous name abroad. Rainfall is heavy and seasonal, rivers numerous, and the coastline is a mix of natural deep-water harbors and mangrove shallows, a combination that made the continent, from the first foreign ships to reach it, an unusually easy place to land, and an unusually hard one to leave once its resources became known.

Kyrite deposits run through the Kyresh Range and its foothills in visible, sometimes surface-exposed seams, glowing faintly even unworked, a fact that made Kyre's mineral wealth obvious to outside prospectors within a single generation of first contact, long before anyone bothered to properly document who already lived there, or ask them.

## 1. The Kyrai

### Physical Nature

The Kyrai are, functionally and by every measurable account, exactly what early foreign chroniclers dismissively called them: half-animal, half-human, though the phrase itself, still used casually in several colonial holdings, is considered by most Kyrai a foreigner's crude shorthand for something they never needed a special word for at home. Every Kyrai carries visible traits, ears, a tail, sometimes fur-patterning along the forearms or nape, tied to one of several dozen recognized animal lineages, alongside an otherwise fully human frame, gait, and hand. The traits breed true within a lineage and are, as far as any Kyrai account is concerned, no more remarkable a fact of ancestry than eye color is anywhere else.

### Lineages

Kyrai society organizes, in large part, around **lineage**: which animal ancestry a person's traits and, traditionally, their name-line descend from. A lineage functions as something between an extended clan and a guild, carrying its own customary skills, land-claims, and social expectations, though intermarriage between lineages has always been common and unremarkable. Wolf-, deer-, hawk-, and river-otter-lineage Kyrai are among the most numerous and widely spread; smaller, more localized lineages exist throughout the interior, some numbering only a few thousand.

Before the conquest, no single lineage held continent-wide dominance, and Kyre had never been unified under one government, a fact colonial administrators would later, with no apparent sense of irony, cite as evidence the continent lacked "real civilization" worth respecting, even as those same administrators struggled for generations to fully subdue a population organized this loosely.

### Society and Daily Life, Before

Pre-conquest Kyrai society was built around semi-permanent forest settlements, seasonal hunting and gathering rounds, and a well-developed tradition of small-scale kyrite working, jewelry, tools, and minor charms, done with a level of care and craft that early foreign traders, before the trade turned to conquest, admired and paid well for. Kyrai spiritual life centers heavily on lineage-ancestors and on the forest itself as a living, attentive presence, a set of beliefs still practiced, quietly and under real risk, throughout the occupied coast today.

## 2. Kyrite

### What It Is

Kyrite is a crystalline mineral, found in appreciable quantity almost nowhere else in the world, that holds and slowly releases raw magical potential in a form usable by trained casters without the years of personal cultivation most other magical traditions require. A modest kyrite shard, properly cut, can power a working that would otherwise demand a skilled mage's full effort, which is precisely what makes it, to outside eyes, the most valuable export material on the planet.

### Properties and Use

Unworked kyrite glows faintly, a soft color that varies by seam and is used locally to grade quality before any foreign assayer arrives to do the same. Worked kyrite is used abroad in everything from minor enchanted tools to, in its highest grades, components for large-scale magical workings that would otherwise be prohibitively difficult, a use foreign powers have been notably eager to expand and notably reluctant to explain in detail to the people whose land it's pulled from.

Extended, close contact with unrefined kyrite is not harmless. Miners and cutters exposed for years develop a condition known abroad, with characteristic detachment, as **stonelung**, and known among the Kyrai by an older name that translates roughly to "the crystal's debt": a slow deadening of sensation, first in the extremities, that in advanced cases spreads to dull a person's magical or emotional responsiveness altogether. It is well documented, well understood by every mining concession's own physicians, and, in every occupied territory, treated as an acceptable operating cost rather than a reason to change how the mines are run.

## 3. The Conquest

### First Contact to Occupation

Foreign ships first reached Kyre's coast several generations ago, initially as traders, and the earliest recorded exchanges describe cautious commerce: kyrite and crafted goods traded for foreign metal tools and cloth. What changed, within roughly a generation, was the scale of kyrite's value once it reached foreign markets, and the speed with which trading concerns realized that *buying* kyrite from the Kyrai was a far worse arrangement, from a purely extractive standpoint, than simply taking the land it came from and the labor needed to pull it out of the ground.

The ideological turn that made this palatable to the conquering powers' own populations back home was neither subtle nor original: an extensive, deliberately cultivated body of writing, sermon, and eventually law classified the Kyrai as beasts in the fullest sense, animals wearing enough of a human shape to be useful, but not owed the legal or moral status of people. **The Continent of Beasts** is not a folk name that calcified over time; it is the direct, intentional output of that classification, still used in official correspondence by every major occupying power to this day.

### The Occupied Coast and the Free Interior

No occupying power has ever fully subdued Kyre's interior, and the continent today is divided, unevenly and often violently contested, between two zones:

| Zone | Character |
|---|---|
| **The occupied coast** | A ring of foreign-held ports, plantations, and mining concessions along the coastline and lower Kyresh foothills, under direct colonial administration, and the seat of nearly all formal slavery on the continent. |
| **The free interior** | The continent's forested and mountainous heart, never successfully occupied, home to the majority of Kyre's population, organized under traditional lineage governance, and the base from which most resistance to the coastal occupation is organized and supplied. |

## 4. Bondage and the Extraction Economy

### Legal Status

Within every occupied territory, Kyrai are, by the law of whichever foreign power administers that stretch of coast, property rather than persons, a status justified by, and directly downstream of, the "beast" classification. This status is inherited: a child born to an enslaved Kyrai parent within occupied territory is, by the same laws, born enslaved. Manumission exists on paper in some jurisdictions and is, in practice, rare enough to function mostly as a legal fiction used to justify the system's supposed fairness to outside critics.

### Labor

The overwhelming majority of enslaved Kyrai labor is bound directly to the kyrite economy: mining and cutting in the Kyresh foothill concessions, and, to a lesser extent, agricultural labor on coastal plantations growing food for the mining camps and export goods for the wider foreign market. Mine labor is, unsurprisingly, the most sought-after posting for an overseer to avoid assigning themselves and the most lethal for the person assigned to it, given stonelung's slow, well-documented progression and the concessions' near-uniform unwillingness to rotate workers out before symptoms are severe.

Kyrai skill in fine kyrite-working, developed over generations before the conquest, was not lost in the transition to slavery; it was simply expropriated. The most technically demanding cutting and grading work in every major concession is still overwhelmingly done by enslaved Kyrai artisans, whose pre-conquest craft tradition is, without any acknowledgment, the entire technical foundation the export trade runs on.

### Family, Marking, and Sale

Enslaved Kyrai are bought, sold, and transported between concessions with no binding legal recognition of family ties, and separation of families by sale, while not universal, is common enough to be a defining, openly discussed feature of life in the occupied coast, spoken of among the Kyrai with a bitterness that has never dulled with time. Ownership is typically marked by a branded or tattooed lineage-and-owner mark, a practice made crueler in local understanding by its deliberate placement near a person's ear or tail-base, the exact features that once marked their belonging to a lineage and a family rather than to a ledger.

## 5. Resistance and the Free Interior

### The Interior as Refuge and Base

The free interior is not simply where the conquest failed to reach; it is an active refuge, and every occupied territory sits within reach of it. Escape into the interior, when successful, is understood universally among the Kyrai as a return rather than a flight into the unknown, and interior lineages maintain a long, well-organized tradition of receiving, sheltering, and eventually integrating escapees, a practice occupying administrations have never managed to fully map or interdict despite generations of trying.

### Organized Resistance

Beyond individual escape, the border margin between occupied coast and free interior hosts sustained, organized resistance, ranging from small-scale sabotage of mining operations to coordinated raids intended specifically to free labor gangs and disrupt kyrite shipments before they reach port. These efforts are neither uniform nor centrally directed, coordinated loosely between interior lineages and, increasingly in recent memory, sympathetic contacts within the occupied territories themselves, but they are persistent enough that every major concession budgets, openly, for the cost of raids as a routine operating expense, alongside stonelung and equipment wear.

### Cultural Survival

Even within the occupied coast, lineage identity, language, and ancestor-practice have not been extinguished, despite generations of active suppression; they persist quietly, in private, at real risk, and are widely understood among the Kyrai themselves as a form of resistance in their own right, no less real for being unarmed. Foreign administrators who assume generations of bondage have produced a population without a living culture of its own are, without exception, wrong, and have simply never been shown it.

---

## 6. Kyre in the Wider World

Kyrite's value has made Kyre's occupation immensely profitable for the handful of foreign powers holding concessions there, and immensely convenient for every other foreign market that buys the finished product. The moral question is not, in fact, unraised abroad; a small but growing body of foreign critics, merchants, scholars, and clergy alike, has begun openly disputing the "beast" classification and the trade built on it, though these voices remain, so far, a minority against the sheer scale of money the concessions generate, and against powers with every incentive to keep asking the question quietly and answering it slowly.

Nowhere is any of this framed, on the Kyrai side, as an open question at all.
